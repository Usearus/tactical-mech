# Storyboard

Working story for the campaign. Raw ideas and the critique stay in `story_notes.md`. Decisions land here once they are settled. Open questions stay marked instead of being filled with a guess.

## Locked

### Names

Citadel is the government security organization.

Helion is the firm that developed the project and told two stories about it. Shepherd used to work there.

Osterveld is the buyer: the company with the foreign site and the private army.

The project is called the Ledger project in files and in Citadel's internal order. People may still call the machine the system.

Mara is the communications specialist. The squad calls her Quill.

Jacob is the contact Mara trusts. Jacob knows where the unmarked mechs are.

Holt is Mara's former boss.

Commander Mercer is the Citadel officer Bastion trusts. Mercer helped write the cover story and does not open the vault.

### Scope

One campaign path. A small cast. The big turns are written: Shepherd gives up the archive, the pilots were locked in, and Mercer helped write the cover story. The player can lean in a conversation. That lean does not move anyone's loyalty, change what the squad keeps, or open a second path.

Revelations change what earlier missions meant. The story is there so the player cares about the squad and finishes the campaign. It does not carry the weight of a branching tactical RPG.

### What the story is about

Citadel and Helion look like enemies. Their fight is useful to Osterveld, which arranges intel and incentives so that a larger war is the likely outcome. Osterveld uses that war to keep its own forces ahead, to make governments dependent on its systems, and to watch real people decide under fire.

### Ending

The squad breaks Osterveld's command node. Osterveld's forces stop answering. That army is finished. It does not fall back to another base.

In that same fight, Raven releases the cover-up and the recording, and the player sees the public story change. The squad is alive after the release. Comet did not stop it. Bastion, finished with Mercer, stands with it. They have been fugitive until this moment. They are not fugitive after it.

What the public receives is the cover-up and the recording. Citadel sent them, told them the targets were machines, and called them murderers after the body count. The recording shows the pilots were alive and unable to get out. The public does not learn how the system sealed them in. That omission is a choice the squad finishes. Publishing the method would hand the next institution the weapon.

At the Citadel vault, before this fight, they destroy the operational record: the part that sealed pilots in, and the part that kept Raven and Comet on the network so the system could keep learning from them. Shepherd does not carry the rest of that copy out.

The last scene is an internal order to continue the Ledger project with what Citadel has left. The order is real. The copy is incomplete. The scene after that order is the squad, together. Citadel does not explain itself to the public, and the public is not asked to accept the program.

Citadel's interest shows up in two steps. In the first movement, the squad learns Citadel wanted to steal the AI technology before Helion could finish developing it. In the third movement, at the vault, they learn Citadel already kept research from that raid and means to field its own version. The squad leaves that version without the operational record.

### What the AI is for

Osterveld gave the Ledger project a job: stay ahead of any military that could threaten its position, and keep its own losses low. Shaping wars is how it does that job. It feeds the incentives and the intel that make escalation the likely next move, then meets that war with forces already prepared for it.

The system improves by watching real people decide under fire. A simulation cannot guarantee those choices. Osterveld expected Citadel to send a deniable team, and it expected the lab to be defended. It did not know which way the squad would push. Those choices are the valuable part. The squad was a safe source of that data because they were expendable.

The fights themselves give the squad no hint. They play as a machine hunt. In the second movement, the recording shows the pilots were alive and unable to get out. In the third movement, the squad learns the system had rigged the defenders. The pilots could not communicate and could not escape. They were locked in, and they watched it happen to them.

Citadel still owns the cover-up until Raven's release. Osterveld keeps the recording until the squad takes it to the public. Some of the people who own Osterveld suspect how far the Ledger project goes and accept the results because the outcome favors them.

Citadel ends the campaign still intending to field what it kept. It no longer has the operational record. Live pilots locked in a machine, and the squad's own choices used as training, are the part the squad destroys. Citadel's remaining order calls the leftover national security.

### Opening

Citadel tells the squad to destroy an autonomous facility. The site is run by machines, the target has to be eliminated, and no human pilots are there. The squad is not told this is a theft. The prologue is two missions at that facility. The squad is already alone on the field. The first mission is the outer defense. The second is inside. The count happens at the end of the second.

Across the opening missions the squad kills about two dozen people. During the fighting they do not know that. They believe the targets are autonomous.

They learn it on the ground, before any broadcast. After the fighting they go through the wrecks expecting hardware and find people. The squad counts about two dozen themselves. Citadel is still on the channel, through Quill. Through both missions that channel has been confirming the targets are machines. When the squad reports the bodies, Quill relays the order to hold position. An explanation is coming. Raven says stay on the order. Comet says move. They are still there when the broadcasts name them. They leave before anyone reaches the facility. The prologue ends on that argument and those broadcasts. The next fight is in the first movement. Mercer is not a voice they hear.

The critique's single pilot falling out of a wreck is not the reveal. The reveal is the count.

During the fighting, nothing feels human. The squad can explain every contact as a machine. In the second movement, the recording shows the pilots were alive and unable to get out. In the third movement, the squad learns the system rigged those machines so the pilots could not communicate or escape. The pilots were locked in, and they watched it happen.

### Raven and Comet

Raven wants the story public. The truth should clear the squad and put Citadel on record.

Comet wants the squad somewhere safe. Going public, in Comet's view, only makes them easier to kill.

That argument starts at the facility, while they are waiting on the explanation. Raven says stay on the order. Comet says move. They are still there when the broadcasts name them. Comet does not get them out in time.

They keep working together. Neither of them is simply wrong. The player hears the argument between missions. Comet does not stop the release. Raven makes it in the fourth movement anyway.

The public correction is real, and the player sees it land. Raven releases the cover-up and the recording. The squad does not publish how the pilots were sealed in. The squad stays fugitive until that release, and is alive after it. Comet does not stop the release. Bastion stands with it.

### What Citadel intended

Citadel chose a deniable squad and meant to disown them if the raid went bad. It did not plan a massacre headline. After the squad counted the bodies, someone at Citadel decided the squad would be the cover story.

Osterveld expected that kind of operation and that kind of mess. It kept the recording. It did not write the headline. Citadel owns the cover-up.

### The squad

Raven and Comet are the two squad leaders. The other pilots already served under one of them. They stay minor. They start from their leader's side, and a few of them can disagree with that leader more than once. Those disagreements come back. They are still one squad and one path.

Barrage and Bastion served under Raven. Lancer served under Comet.

Barrage stays with Raven. Lancer stays with Comet. They echo their leader.

Bastion starts on Raven's side and breaks with Raven more than once. Bastion wants the evidence given to Commander Mercer. Bastion argues for that meeting across the campaign. The mission where they have to fight beside Mercer is where the argument becomes unavoidable, and where Bastion learns the truth. Mercer helped make the squad the cover story after the body count. Mercer knew. Raven wants the evidence on the public record instead. Comet wants the squad gone before Mercer can betray them.

Quill does not carry a separate argument across the campaign. In the prologue, she is the relay. Citadel's orders reach the squad through her, including the order to hold. She believes that explanation is coming. She passes the order on in good faith. The broadcasts hit her at the same time as the pilots.

Shepherd is one person. The notes spell that name Shepard. This storyboard uses Shepherd, which is the name on the pilot.

Shepherd is not with the squad in the prologue, and not at the shutdown. The squad finds Shepherd later. Shepherd is a former Helion employee who lost their position and their money, gives the squad information, and helps them reach a research facility. Shepherd is not a squad leader, and the pilots who already served under Raven or Comet do not shift that loyalty to Shepherd.

Once Shepherd has joined, Shepherd wants the archive: proof that Helion lied about its technology, even if getting it risks the squad. That want gives way when keeping the archive would cost the squad. Shepherd has the chance to take the file at the others' expense, and does not take it. After that, Shepherd's past explains what they know, and Shepherd chooses the people. When Raven and Comet argue, Shepherd picks a side in the moment.

### Campaign shape

The campaign is a prologue and four movements.

Prologue. Two missions at the same facility. Citadel tells the squad to destroy an autonomous facility. The site is run by machines, the target has to be eliminated, and no human pilots are there. The squad is not told this is a theft. The squad is already alone on the field. The first mission is the outer defense and makes the briefing feel true. The second mission is inside the facility. At the end of the second, they walk the wrecks and count about two dozen people. Quill relays the order to hold position, and believes the explanation is coming. While they wait, Raven says stay on the order and Comet says move. They are still there when the broadcasts name them. They leave before anyone reaches the facility. The next fight is in the first movement. Mercer is not on the channel.

First movement. It opens with the shutdown, then follows the original mission order.

The other pilots on the field are Barrage, Bastion, and Lancer. Shepherd is not there yet. Their mechs are shut down remotely, one by one. Raven's and Comet's mechs have already been pulled off that network, so the shutdown misses them. The squad sees both results and cannot tell them apart. The two leads keep their mechs and tell the others to meet them at a safe house. Raven and Comet are getting the others there. They are not hunting a mission record on the way.

Citadel's network is what shuts the other mechs down. The system is what had already unlocked the leads. It wanted Raven and Comet still making choices in the field. The squad does not learn that until the third movement.

After the safe house, the missions run in this order:

1. Find Jacob, and discover new equipment. Jacob is someone Mara already trusts, and not Holt. Quill gets the squad to Jacob. Jacob knows where the mechs are. The equipment includes mechs for Barrage, Bastion, and Lancer. Those machines are not on Citadel's network. Raven and Comet keep the ones they already have.
2. Recover that equipment from a warehouse. They are attacked twice. Both attacks are Holt's people. The first finds the squad at the warehouse. The second tries to stop the mechs leaving.
3. Fight Holt, face to face. Holt comes after them for going to Jacob. Holt knew the squad was deniable and believes a failed off-books raid has to be buried. Holt does not know the murder headline was written after the body count, and does not know about the theft. Commander Mercer is a different person.
4. Shepherd finds the squad after the fight with Holt. The broadcasts named them, and Shepherd comes looking. The squad was not searching for a former Helion employee. Jacob is not involved. Shepherd knows a second Helion facility, not the one where the squad counted the bodies, and wants the archive there. Shepherd gives them information and helps them get there.
5. Fight through that facility. The defenders are Helion's own people. Citadel is not there. Osterveld is not a force on the field.
6. Fight Helion. This is where they reach the records. The Ledger project was already being fielded. Citadel had been told it was still unfinished, which is why Citadel tried to steal it before it was finished. Osterveld had been told it was ready, and that version matches the records. The archive names Osterveld. The squad does not yet know Osterveld is the company that has been pushing the war. They also learn the operation was part of a larger fight. They were not hunting that motive from the safe house. They do not know it during the prologue. The records are at Shepherd's facility, not in the wrecks they already left. The squad does not yet learn what the fielded units do to the people inside them.

Second movement. The squad follows the name Osterveld from the archive to a facility outside the country. They believe Osterveld is building a private military with the technology. Osterveld's own military holds the site. Local security is also there, told to guard a civilian facility. Those local troops are not part of the conspiracy. They know the public story. They think they are stopping the squad that murdered those pilots. Osterveld lets that story do the work. The squad has to go through them to reach Osterveld's part of the site. There the squad discovers Osterveld has been pushing both sides toward the war, and that Osterveld kept the recording of the opening. The recording shows the pilots were alive and unable to get out. The squad does not yet know what sealed them in, and does not yet know the system was pushing Helion's two pitches.

Third movement, first mission. Bastion gets the meeting with Commander Mercer. Osterveld's forces hit that meeting. The squad and Mercer fight together to survive, and the cover story comes out in that mission. Bastion learns Mercer helped write it. During that ambush the squad learns where Citadel keeps the research from the original raid. Osterveld's strike orders name the vault. The squad takes that off the attackers. Mercer may know the place and still does not open it. Mercer does not take them there and does not give them a key. That mission ends there.

Third movement, second mission. The squad breaks into that Citadel site. The stolen operational record shows the system doing the job it was given, the opening as useful data, the lock-in, the shutdown list, and the two pitches the system pushed while Osterveld went along. The site itself shows Citadel means to field that research. Shepherd does not carry the rest of that copy out. The squad destroys the operational record there: the part that sealed pilots in, and the part that kept Raven and Comet on the network.

Fourth movement. The squad breaks Osterveld's command node, a site they have not been to before. The foreign site was where they found the recording. The Citadel vault was where they learned the system and destroyed its operational record. This node is what Osterveld's forces answer to. Those forces stop answering. That army is finished. Raven releases the cover-up and the recording as that fight happens, and the player sees the public story change. The public sees the lie and the pilots alive, unable to get out. The public does not learn how the system sealed them in. The squad has been fugitive until this point, and is alive after the release. The last scene is an internal order to continue with the incomplete copy, and then the squad, together. Citadel does not explain itself to the public.
